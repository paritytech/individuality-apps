# The game report document

One document per game in the "DIM2 Game Reports" artifact: collection **games**,
doc_id **g<N>** (for example g160). The page renders whatever is there, newest
game first, with trend sparklines across games. A real, trimmed example is next
to this file: example-report-g160.json.

## Fields

- **game** (number), **env** (the environment tag), **kickoff** (ISO, UTC), **summary** (the TL;DR: bold section headings after a blank line, bullets below; see the worked example below)
- **dataAsOf** (stored reports only): the manifest's local CEST/CET snapshot time. Live reports omit it; the page renders it as “Telemetry as of …” in the header metadata.
- **players[]**, one per player:
  - **username** (Sentry user.username; shown instead of the key), **id** (account address)
  - **device**, **os**, **host** (for example "Polkadot App 0.10.0"), **hostBuild** (the build number in brackets, for example "44")
  - **signup**, **join**, **game**, **payoff**, **draw**: each is {outcome, ms, note?} or null; the page shows them as Registration, Join, Game, Chest and Prize draw. **draw** comes from the payoff journey's draw.show step and draw.invited / draw.won: won, shown or not shown, noted "in the draw" or "not in the draw". The Registration cell (signup) keeps the journeys' own outcome, says where each ran ("from the chat", "from the app"), reads "broadcast" when only a signup-broadcast event exists, and, when the chain registered the account but no journey ended landed or sent, adds "registered on chain HH:MM CEST (or CET) chain time (<call>); success not observed" (block time runs about 30 s behind the phones). **surface** is chat, app, or both comma-joined, observed from the journeys. A registered player with no sign-up journey reads "no sign-up attempt in Sentry; registered on chain HH:MM CEST (or CET) chain time (<call>)": the route the chain reader gives (web or other) is inferred from the call name, so no surface is claimed
  - **registration** (script, registered accounts only): {at, route, call}. at is the ISO time of the block that registered the account, call the Game.sign_up_* call in it, route "web" for sign_up_with_account_lite_invite (the only route the web app implements) and "other" for any other (a native client: sign_up_with_invite under GameAsInvited, …). The chain reader (chain.json) holds registeredAt, registeredBlock, registrationCall and registrationOrigin (PeopleLiteAuth, GameAsInvited, …) per account
  - **telemetry**: full, partial or none. A non-zero queue-drop count in **telemetryHealth** forces partial even when a vote trail exists.
  - **telemetryHealth** / **telemetryNote**: the app's delivery-health counters and a human-readable "Partial due to telemetry throttling" note when events or metric envelopes were dropped.
  - **bot**: the e2e- test.tag when the player is a scripted run (any event carries one), else null. Bots stay in the funnel, join, vote trails and report filing, and are left out of smoothness, video and the device and host text (shown as "bot"); the "bots" fact lists them
  - **mode**: low power when any metric row of the player has lowpower on, full when the rows have only off, null when no row has the tag (an older build or no metrics)
  - **modeReason**: why low power came on, from the lowpower.reason tag of the rows with lowpower on ("probe weak", "probe weak confirmed", "probe no verdict", "debug menu", "url", "manual"); null for a full player or an older build. The smoothness row label shows it, for example "low power (debug menu)"
  - **probe**: the device probe, {p95Ms, medianMs (v3), frames, version, thresholdMs}, from ui.probe_frame_ms and ui.probe_frames. From v2 it also has samples, rule, borderlineFromMs and weakFromMs. From v5 it also has **stage** (countdown or late) and **attempts** (the samples that ended on a hidden page). These two are null on older builds. A reload gives one probe per page load, so a text field can hold two values, comma-joined ("countdown, late"). "none" when the rows carry the tags but there is no probe point (?lowpower=1 or a v5 probe with no sample; on older builds also a late join or a page hidden during the probe), null when no row has the tags
  - **join.peerAllMs**: how long until every peer was connected
  - **video**: rttP50, rttP95, lossP95 (worst moments of the worst pair-round), lossP50 (typical loss: median pair-round), lossWorstPairP50 (typical loss of that same worst pair-round; kept, no longer quoted), jitterP95, inFps, outFps, inHeight, outHeight, outKbps, freezeMs, path, limited, plus **worstRtt**, **worstLoss** and **worstFreeze**, which independently retain the account, peer, round, path and rendered pair label belonging to that value (freeze names the receiving direction), and:
    - **table[]**: the Video quality table, {metric, unit, better (lower or higher), stat, samples, samplesLabel, p50, p75, p95, worst}. Percentiles are nearest-rank over pair-rounds (each pair-round's median: rtc.*{stat:p50}); signalling over each phone-peer row's median; frozen video over every pair-round with 0 for a clean one; frames per second and picture height over the link directions both phones reported (below). P75 and P95 are the values 75 % and 95 % were at least as good as: the high tail for times, loss and freezes, the low tail for frames, height and upload. Worst for round-trip time, loss and jitter is rttP95 / lossP95 / jitterP95 (the worst pair-round's 95th percentile), the figure the summary quotes; report-lint checks it.
    - **worstLink**: {fps, height}, each {sent, received, round, samples}: the link direction with the lowest received value and what its sender sent.
    - **codecKinds**: {samples, samplesLabel, encoding, decoding, byPlatform[]}, each {hardware, software, unsupported}, from the rtc.link rows' rtc.encoder_kind / rtc.decoder_kind, counted on the same link samples as the Links routes (one per phone per peer per round that carried a round-trip time, joined on account, round and rtc.peer; rtc.link rows for seats whose player never came carry no video and are left out: 36 of 140 in game 254); byPlatform splits them by the player's OS. A link whose coder changed mid-round has a row per kind; it counts once, as **mixed** ("switched mid-round"), in any row order. In game 254 iOS reported nothing (81 of 104) and all 23 Android link samples said hardware.
    - **pairing**: {rule, samples, partial, receivedAboveSent} per kind (fps, height): how many link directions both phones reported, how many of them one phone measured only partly (its game.round.N step under 90 % of that round's median: a reload or a late open; marked partial on each direction and kept, since dropping them hid the worst links), how many have an unknown measured span (**spanUnknown**: a phone with no game.round.N step for that round, whose game journey never finished; nothing is flagged when no phone has round steps), and how many read received above sent ({links, partialLinks, maxGap, maxGapWhole}). Both sides are the same statistic, the median of the round's 3 s readings, each over its own phone's round step; the readings never leave the phone, so the two sides cannot be lined up second by second. In game 254, 7 frame-rate and 2 height directions read received above sent: 6 partial (5 from deedee.08's 13.6 s round 1 after a reload), and 3 whole-round from one iPhone sender whose ladder moved between lean and survival in round 2, up to 8 frames per second apart.
    - Link directions: rtc.in_* and rtc.out_* rows joined on **rtc.pair** (the same id on both ends of a link) and round; the sender of an out_* row is the reporting phone, of an in_* row the other end of the pair. Only directions both phones reported count, so sent and received compare the same links. They come from a query of their own (linkPairs in the pull), so the metrics query's grouping, and every older figure, stays as it was.
  - **adaptations**: structured report-window evidence about performance adaptation. **lowPower** counts low-power/full/unknown players and observed reasons; **picture** contains received/sent height and frames-per-second distributions over every link direction both phones reported (scope "link directions both phones reported"; see video.pairing), bucketed at the upload ladder's values (360 / 240 / 180 / 120 px; 24 / 18 / 12 / 8 fps, a rate within 1 counting as that rate); any other value gets a bucket of its own with offLadder true, labelled with its cause where the evidence shows one (a height at a ladder height over the square root of 2, such as 254 px, is "the browser's own downscale of 360, half the pixels; not a ladder step": in game 254 three iPhones sent it while their ladder step was full or lean); **sendLadder** ({senders, steppedDown, lowestRung[], secondsByRung[], ladder}) is how the upload ladder behaved, from the rtc.send_adaptation.round transactions (measurement_set rungs-network, numeric measurements rtc.send.rung_<step>_s; max_rung is the lowest step reached), null on builds without it; **qualityLimits** counts links, senders, reasons and raw seconds from rtc.quality_limited_s; **signals** contains TURN route counts, rtc.heal_ms events, game.rtc.heal/reconnect/stall step counts, freezes, and worst encode/decode p95 values. Low power affects visual effects, not video size. Each phone's upload adapts on a five-step ladder (full, lean, low, minimum, survival) when its upload bandwidth runs short; the browser's encoder can lower size or frame rate further on its own.
  - **smoothness[]**: {scene, p50, p95, slowPct}. A low power player is in no pool: each one gets rows of its own, labeled "low power" (for example "c.01 (iPhone, low power) · game.round")
  - **steps[]**: {step, ms}
- **knownPlatformErrors**: explicit counts for platform-bugs #76 (subscription dropped / metadata block selection / unknown follow subscription, the wording from host build 60), #77 (the 120 s TrUAPI timeout on wire 3,5, unpin_head, only: other wire methods' timeouts are not that bug), counted from registration opening (bounded to 2 h before kickoff only when registration opened over 24 h earlier, a multi-day game), and #78 (not pinned / BlockNotPinnedError, with call site). Occurrences merge duplicate saved-log and Sentry copies within two seconds and retain player, device, host build, release and local time. Every absent kind is printed as zero; each non-zero kind creates a warn issue naming its platform-bugs number.
- **payoffFacts**: label to text
- **pullGaps[]** (script): the Sentry queries that still failed after their retries (429, 5xx and network errors are retried with a growing pause), {query, players} with players null for a game-wide query (tagged accounts, coverage builds). The pull goes on without them; each gap is a "- Gap: the <query> query failed for N players; their numbers are missing." line under Telemetry, a "Partial Sentry pull" issue (bad), and a red line on the page. The pull records them per player as **queryErrors** {query: message} and game-wide as **pullErrors**. Every paged query sorts stably (sentry.stable_sort: the grouping fields of a grouped query, timestamp and id of a row query). Numeric tracemetrics aggregates fetch raw points in pages of 1000 under timestamp + id order, dedupe by id, check the resulting row count against a separate count(value), retry the raw fetch once on a shortfall, then record a gap. The complete raw points are aggregated by the same local function for live and stored reports (game 254 previously lost 5 points when Sentry paged grouped rows). The 30-day slice release lookup is the set-only exception: it retains one checked grouped query plus the store's frozen context table instead of fetching more than 100,000 raw points whose values are never published.
- **funnelTable.intro**: the line above the game funnel table (Ionut's item 19): who telemetry was picked up from in the window, and which of them the table follows. It replaces the old chat-only side line: funnelTable.side keeps the count and accounts, no text.
- **issues[]**: {severity (bad, warn or note), title, detail, action}
- **links** (optional): the output of scripts/game-links.py for this game, as is: **players**, **rounds**, **matrix**, **timeline**, **events** (see SKILL.md, Link picture). Each player records whether the report funnel says they joined: any telemetry from or about them during the game, or a report on chain. Pairs involving someone without that presence evidence use the neutral **never-joined** state and do not count toward coverage. The page draws a matrix per round group and a per-link timeline from it. Also saved as docs-internal/game-logs/game-<N>/links.json.
- **coverage** (script, null without chain or usable viewer telemetry): where the missing video coverage went: {coverage, measured, na, missing, webOnly, nonWeb, clipped, oneSided, coverageErrors, causes[]}, causes = {cause, pct, pairs[], items[]} by pct descending. pct is percent of the coverage metric (never "points"). coverage is the mean over expected pair-rounds whose viewers sent game telemetry. A missing expected direction is zero when that viewer has game roots or metrics; only a viewer with no game telemetry, or whose dedicated coverage query failed, is unavailable. Two available directions use the lower; one is used and counted in oneSided; neither is n/a. rtc.video_ms is divided by the lag-adjusted chain play-window duration. Sums are always capped at the chain window; clipped counts only skew beyond one 3 s detection tick. Missing is 100 minus coverage, with causes diagnosed from the link timeline/logs: "Native players", "App in the background", "Page reloaded mid-round", "Late open", "Unknown, no telemetry", "Link never came up", "Round N link setup", or "Other". Logs never supply the number. coverageErrors counts per-viewer query failures and the note discloses them. Each pair-round weighs 100/measured percent. pairs[] names players and is for analysis only; items[] hold the numbers the note's sentences come from. webOnly is the mean coverage between two web players, nonWeb is true when a non-web player reported
- **coverageNote** (script, null when there is no coverage): the text the page shows under the video coverage box, generated from coverage by coverage_note(). Never names a player. Shape:

      Where the missing 43 % goes:
      - Native players: 41.4 %. The 12 web-to-native pair-rounds had no video.
      - App in the background: 1.4 %. One player's app came back 10 s after round 1 started (same session, silent in between); ...
      - Other: 0.1 %. Fractions of a second at round edges.
      Between web players alone: 97 %.

  The header's number is 100 minus the page's integer coverage; the cause values add up to the missing share to 0.1 %; the last line is "Every player was on the web app." when no non-web player took part. Do not rewrite it by hand except to correct a wrong cause, and check the causes against the game logs first.
- **timesNote**: one line saying which time zone the text uses
- **facts[]** (written by scripts/game-report.py): {kind, text, ...} the notable mechanical findings (late confirm-screen overrides, reporters with no telemetry, backgrounded reporters, clock skews over 30 s, other builds, "lost membership" on candidates, vote defaults, chain ties). A starting checklist for the summary and issues, not the limit of what to look at. The page ignores it.
- **funnel[]** (script): the account-level game path and its evidence. Its first row carries the three mutually exclusive entry paths (opened the app, signed up from chat without opening the app, and on chain only); later rows carry {stage, count, accounts, dropped}. The registered row also carries the same three source buckets.
- **funnelTable** (script): the phone-first presentation model derived from funnel: {entryTotal, entries[], rows[], side}. Counts in entries add to entryTotal. Rows carry Stage / Players / Of N / Dropped / Why data, grouped drop reasons with expandable account lists, registered source buckets, a collapsible six-step no-drop run, and n/a for unmeasured stages. side is the separate count of accounts that only had DIM2 chat running.
- **notificationFunnel** (script): the per-message notification steps and side exclusions. The page renders these as the same Stage / Players / Of N / Dropped / Why table layout as funnelTable.

## Conventions

- **Per-player rows carry the username.** Label shared lists as "theender.02 · game.round" so one list can hold every player.
- **The video block is the worst value across players and peers**, except rttP50 and outKbps, which are the group median. Say so in payoffFacts.
- **All times in text are Central European time**, spelled out: CEST (UTC+2) in summer, CET (UTC+1) in winter. Sentry returns UTC, so convert. Only kickoff stays ISO UTC.
- **Never guess a username or a host build.** Leave the field null and add an issue (severity note) that says it is missing and who can supply it. The app learns the name only on the results card, and iOS hosts send no build number.
- **Be thorough.** Every player in the room key (even one with no telemetry), every journey, every failure event, the payoff facts, and video and frame times per player.
- **Filter by game.index, never by environment.** Add each player's earlier journeys by user.id inside the window.
- **Leave out scripted runs** (test.tag e2e-*).

## How it is made

Run scripts/game-report.py (SKILL.md, Write a game report). It fills every field except
summary and issues: the player rows, funnel, groups, vote trails, failures, video, smoothness, steps,
payoffFacts, links and facts. The rest of this file is what each field means.

## Writing it

Keep **report.json** as the full local evidence record. Publish only **report-publish.json** and
**report-links.json**. Render them first through **scripts/local_report_preview.py**, inspect the
ordinary report and **#links-<N>** screenshot view, and do not show Ionut an uninspected render.
Republish the page source before its data contract. Then read both current game-document versions
and use one ArtifactData batch set: **report-publish.json** as **g<N>** and **report-links.json** as
**g<N>-links**. Never overwrite an older reviewed judgment with a placeholder.

## Worked example: the TL;DR of game 254

Ionut's layout, 16 Oct 2026 (it replaces the nested bullets of the game 206 example). The script writes all of it except the Claude review slot. Shape: the headline line; then for each section a blank line, the section name in bold on its own line, and plain "- " bullets under it, never nested. Sections in order: Players, Telemetry, Video, Links, Performance adaptations, Claude review. CEST only, no acronyms, "%" never "points", counts that add up, "n/a" where there is no data. Players opens with who telemetry came from (item 19: the chat-only accounts and the ones the counts follow), then the waterfall from the accounts that used the app or signed up, then where they dropped out (counts only). Packet loss is one figure per pair: typical pair, worst pair (its worst moments). The bandwidth line gives totals and says it is the browser's own encoder limit; the upload ladder line says it is the app's own five steps; names stay on the page. Mechanics and code details go in issues[], not here. The breakdown of the missing coverage is coverageNote, which the script writes; its lines are repeated under Video.

- 29 players → 21 used the app or signed up → 18 registered → 14 joined → 14 finished.
- Where they dropped out: 3 didn't register, 4 registered but never joined.

**Telemetry**
- 14 of 14 reporters have their complete end-of-game log in Sentry.
- Sentry rate-limit counters are present for 14 reporters; they record 0 refused or dropped uploads.

**Video**
- Coverage 82 %.
- ...

**Links**
- How links connected, counted once per phone per peer per round (104 link samples, so a pair can count twice): STUN 58, TURN 40, Direct 6.
- Video round-trip time between the two phones: 104 ms for the typical pair, 1,149 ms for the worst pair.
- Packets lost: typical pair 0 %, worst pair 45.2 % (at its worst moments).
- Lowest rates, on the 104 link directions both phones reported (one phone sending to another in one round; 28 of them partly measured, because one phone reloaded or opened late): the link with the lowest frame rate received 9 frames per second while its sender sent 8; the link with the smallest picture received 120 pixels high while its sender sent 120. Received reads above sent on 7 frame-rate directions (4 partly measured; 3 over the whole round, by at most 8 frames per second) and 2 picture-height directions (both partly measured). Each phone sends one median per round, so the two sides can't be lined up second by second.
- The worst pair-round had 22.2 s of frozen video.
- The browser's own encoder lowered the video of 13 senders because their upload bandwidth was short (the browser's built-in limit, separate from our upload ladder below), 881 s in total across their links; none for the processor.
- Hardware or software video coding: most phones don't report it (81 of 104 link samples); 23 of the 23 Android link samples reported hardware encoding and decoding, 0 software, 0 said nothing; iOS reports nothing (81).

**Performance adaptations**
- 5 of the 14 players ran in low power mode: 4 because the device check found the phone slow, 1 because a second check confirmed it.
- Our upload ladder (the app's own five steps, full to survival): 7 of 14 senders stepped down at least once (lowest step reached: 4 to lean, 2 to low, 1 to survival). Time on each step, all senders and rounds: full 1,499 s, lean 168 s, low 105 s, minimum 36 s, survival 42 s.
- Picture height in pixels, on the same 104 link directions as under Links: received 82 at 360, 10 at 254 (the browser's own downscale of 360, half the pixels; not a ladder step), 9 at 240, 2 at 180, 1 at 120; sent 80 at 360, 12 at 254 (the browser's own downscale of 360, half the pixels; not a ladder step), 9 at 240, 2 at 180, 1 at 120.
- Frames per second, on the same 104 link directions: received 95 at 24, 1 at 22 (not a ladder value), 5 at 18, 2 at 12, 1 at 8; sent 92 at 24, 4 at 18, 1 at 15 (not a ladder value), 2 at 14 (not a ladder value), 3 at 12, 2 at 8.

**Claude review (judgment only)**
